# Design system: the sticker book

Kata (型, "form, pattern") is a daily journal with a companion that checks in on you, keeps a map of what you're working on, and people you share the good bits with. The look is a sticker book: warm paper, ink outlines, hard offset shadows, eight saturated hues, chunky condensed type. It replaced the quiet paper-and-seal look (rejected as too plain for everyone, 2026-10-29); don't drift back to it. Stay inside this system; change it here first if it has to change. Visual reference: `components/looks/HomeMock.tsx` (Sticker book) at `/looks`.

## Colour

- Tokens are CSS variables in `app/globals.css`: `--ground` (paper), `--surface` (a card), `--raised` (a recess inside a card), `--ink`, `--edge` (outlines and hard shadows: ink in the light theme, chalk in the dark), `--accent` (vermilion: Kata's seal and the one primary action per view; a primary button fills with `--accent-fill`, a deeper vermilion in the light theme, so its text passes 4.5:1), `--sun` (marker yellow: pressed, selected, the active tab, the writer's own bubble), `--ok` / `--bad` / `--warn` / `--info` (thread statuses), with `--on-accent`, `--on-sun`, `--on-hue`. Utilities: `bg-surface`, `text-ink/70`, `border-edge`, `bg-sun`…
- Eight hues (0 tomato, 1 sun, 2 sky, 3 mint, 4 grape, 5 pink, 6 teal, 7 orange). Put `data-hue={n}` on an element; inside it `bg-hue` is the saturated fill (text `text-on-hue`), `bg-tint` the soft tint and `text-deep` the readable text on that tint. Every thread and every person owns a hue (`thread.hue`, `user.hue`, `hueOf()` in `lib/social/dto.ts`). Colour says who or what; it is never decoration.
- Dark mode is designed, not inverted: chalk outlines on warm charcoal, tints go deep and deep text goes light. It follows the system unless `data-theme` is set on `<html>` (boot script in `app/layout.tsx`; `ThemeToggle` writes it). `.chapter` is the dark book in either theme.
- Contrast: small text is never lighter than `text-ink/65`. On a hue fill use `text-on-hue`; on a tint use `text-deep`.

## Type

- `font-display` is Bricolage Grotesque; `.font-display` makes it 800 weight at 84% width. Headlines, names, numbers, big labels. It has no italic: never write `italic` with it. Emphasise a word with `.mark` (a marker swipe) or `text-accent`.
- `font-sans` is Instrument Sans for everything else: interface, your writing, Kata's messages, letters. There is no serif.
- Sizes: `text-display-xl/-l/-m/-s`, `text-prompt`, `text-prose`, `text-lede`; UI at 1rem / 0.9375rem / 0.875rem, 0.8125rem minimum. Sentence case. No uppercase eyebrows, no mono labels.
- Fit-to-width headlines use container units (`[font-size:calc(100cqi/K)]` inside an `@container`).

## Shape and material

- `.card` is a sticker: surface, 2px `--edge` outline, `rounded-lg` (24px), a 5px hard shadow. Most content sits on cards. Inside a card use `.well` (a raised recess, no outline); never nest a card in a card.
- `.chip` is the pressable pill (outline, 3px shadow; `aria-pressed`, `aria-checked`, `aria-selected` or `aria-current` turns it sun), `.chip-sm` the small one. `.tag` is a small hue label (`data-hue`). `.scale` + `.scale-step` is one row of equal steps (the mood check, reactions, view switches); add `.scale-row` for side-by-side content. `.bubble-kata` (ink) and `.bubble-you` (sun) are the conversation. In it, `.piece` is a thread as a soft patch of its hue (`.piece-past` once settled), `.answers` + `.answer` a question's answers two to a row, `.answered` the one given, `.receipt` something done (soft, never outlined), and `.not-now` the quiet way to put something off. `.tape` is washi tape on a card (`data-hue`). `.control` is every input, `.writing` the borderless writing field, `.ruled` lined paper, `.link` an inline link, `.rule` a hairline colour inside cards (`border-t-2` for structure). `.press` gives buttons the sticker press; its shadow lives in the class so hover and active can move it, so never add a `shadow-*` utility to a `.press` element.
- Buttons: `Button` / `buttonClass` — primary (accent, one per view), secondary (surface), quiet, danger. All pills.
- Radii: `rounded-xs` 8px, `-sm` 12px, `-md` 16px (inputs, wells), `-lg` 24px (cards), `-xl` 32px, `rounded-full` (pills, avatars). Shadows are hard offsets (`shadow-sticker`, `shadow-sticker-sm`) on outlined things only; `shadow-float` only on floating UI.
- Tilt (±1–7°) only for things that are stuck on: map stickers, question cards, tape. Never on text people read at length.

## Motion

- One curve: `ease-out` = `cubic-bezier(0.22, 1, 0.36, 1)` (`EASE_OUT` in `lib/motion.ts`). UI 0.3–0.6s. Stickers press: hover lifts 1px onto a 4px shadow, active pushes 2px into a 1px one. `.appear` for new messages; `.typing` only while Kata is composing or a friend is typing. No idle loops, no bounce. Reduced motion turns everything off.
- Landing motion is GSAP, imported lazily by one director (`components/landing/Scenes.tsx`) and wired to server markup by `data-*` hooks. `gsap.matchMedia` conditions cover every width (`desktop` / `compact`) and `reduce` opts out. The `staged:` variant applies only with JS (`html.js`) and no reduced-motion preference; runways and hidden before-states live behind it. GSAP absorbs CSS `translate`/`rotate`: on anything GSAP moves, let GSAP own centring and rotation.
- Inside the app, Framer Motion is fine for small transitions. The page wrapper in `AppShell` fades opacity only, in CSS (`.page-in`), so a page shows from its first paint rather than once the scripts load: a transform there would trap fixed bars.

## Maru, the seal and people

- The companion inside Kata is Maru by default, and each writer can rename it and choose what it calls them (`User.companionName` / `User.nickname`, empty means the default). Client components read both with `useCompanion()` (set by `AppShell`); server pages use `await namesFor(userId)`; landing and sign-in pages say Maru. A rename (Settings, onboarding's first step, or asking in chat) leaves a `names` receipt in today's conversation and the model's history starts after it, or the old name in it wins. Never hard-code "Maru" on a signed-in screen.
- Each writer also picks the colour it wears (`User.companionHue`: a hue 1–7, null for vermilion; `COMPANION_COLOURS` in `lib/companion/name.ts`). `AppShell` sets `data-maru` on the shell and on `<html>`, and `MaruMark` fills from `--maru`, so every face follows; `MaruColours` is the picker (Settings, onboarding's first step), and `set_names` takes a colour in chat.
- It speaks in `.bubble-kata` beside its face (`MaruMark`). `Seal` / `KataMark` (vermilion with 型 cut out) is Kata's own mark: the logo, a saved page, the 404. A person is an `Avatar`. Keep the three distinct.
- It says hi whenever they come back (`sayHello` in `lib/companion/hello.ts`, from `AskMaru` after 30 minutes away on this device, and never within 20 minutes of the last word): a list that's still going, the day's mood if it's open or the evening's if it hasn't been asked, a thread that's due, or what's next, always with taps. No model, and an unanswered hello is replaced by the next one (`hello: true`), so coming and going stores one message. Not in focus, which asks its own questions, and not while writing a page or a message.
- Work, not talk. Its messages carry receipts (`done`: what a tool did, each opening what it touched) and choices (`choices`: what it could do next), drawn by `Receipts` and `Choices` in `components/home/Work.tsx`. A tapped choice (`do:<i>`, or `do:no` for Not now) runs its stored tool in `runChoice` without the model, and a mood typed as the whole answer to the day's mood question counts as tapped (`moodSaid`), while an "ok" later on stays a word in the conversation. Text is one short line or nothing; a line that only restates a receipt is dropped. Anything that changed refreshes the page it's on (a `refresh` action), so client components that keep their props in state (`ThreadsBoard`, `Memories`) take new props when they arrive.
- Taps are deterministic: the model may pick what to offer, but what a tap does, and every word after it, is code. A thread in the conversation is drawn as itself (`components/maru`): `CheckIn` (its head, then the answers Maru asked for, with a quiet Not now on every one) and `ThreadPiece` (its head and the same `ThreadSteps` its card on home and the Threads page use). Both are drawn from the threads as they are now, not as they were when asked: only the newest piece on a thread can be tapped, and one settled since (done, paused, today counted, archived) shows where it is with nothing to tap. The server holds the same line: an old check-in never reopens or recounts anything (`settledSince`), and a mood is for its own day. A step on a piece goes to `answerStep`; a check-in answer to `answer`; both reply through `threadReply`: their side says which thread ("Finish scrapers: Done"), Maru's has a receipt for what changed (`stepSaid`), at most one short line, and a one-line form where something is worth writing down (what's in the way, on whom, the next step), drawn as the question with its box (`LineForm`). Never a bare "Done.", never "What's next?". A status-only `update_thread` the model offers becomes the thread's piece, once per thread; every other choice is named by what it does (`labelFor`), never by the model's label, so a button can't say one thing and do another. Receipts are soft patches of the hue of what they touched; what can be pressed is outlined.
- It never asks an open question. A choice can carry a form (`inputs`: text with ideas to tap, number with a unit, date, pick, or a short list): when a tool is called without something it needs (no title, nothing to log), `askWithForm` offers the form instead of failing, and `offer_choices` with an argument left out gets one too (`formFor` in `tools.ts`). Complete choices are one tap, with a pen to change them first. `applyInputs` (`lib/companion/forms.ts`) checks what was filled in against the form; a choice that can't run stays open and the writer hears why. Check-ins on a thread that keeps numbers carry a log form, so logging never needs the model; a habit's check-in asks one thing (Done, Not yet, Pause it) and offers its numbers after Done.
- Threads (`lib/db/models/Thread.ts`) are its mental model of you. They are private; only threads marked `shared` appear on a profile, and one shared with friends (`with`, below) is seen by them. `listThreads` is what's current, for Maru and home; the Threads page adds `olderThreads` (archived, and done over two weeks ago), so `?t=` always finds one and Bring back can undo Archive. Anything that claims a profile needs a handle first (`NEEDS_PROFILE`): showing a thread, sharing a finished one, a name on a gallery page. Pages are always private; a moment or a reply is the only thing others see, and only ever as a choice the writer taps: `share_moment` and `draft_replies` make editable drafts, posted by `post_moment` / `post_reply`, which no model can call. A new name or bio is the same: `update_profile` drafts it and `save_profile` saves it on the tap. Orders about private things (a thread, a check-in time, the theme) just happen. A draft with a blank like `[book]` can't be posted until it's filled. A posted reply shows under its post at once (`replied` action, `REPLIED_EVENT`).
- What a thread can be depends on its kind (`stepsFor` in `lib/companion/steps.ts`, shared by the client and the server): a habit is done today or paused, never stuck or finished, Done on one counts the day and Going takes it back; a person is never done. Setting a step is one rule wherever it's set (`stepChange` in `threads.ts`: the thread API, check-ins, pieces). Check-ins (`checkins.ts`) put a date first, on any kind of thread but a habit, which goes by its rhythm (a habit's old date is ignored in check-ins, on the map and in what the model reads): the day itself outranks being stuck, a past date gets "how did it go", a daily habit is asked daily, and at most one stuck or waiting thread takes a slot unless it was promised.
- A thread keeps up to four fields of its own (`Thread.fields`: a number with a unit and an optional daily goal, or words), made up for it: the model passes `track` to `create_thread` / `update_thread`, and `guessFields` (`lib/companion/fields.ts`, client-safe) suggests them from the title when it doesn't (a walk: minutes and km). Numbers keep one value per day (`days`, the last 60). `log_thread` sets a day's values (`add` for "another 15 min"), adds a field it's told about if there's room, and counts a habit as done. The Threads page logs, charts the last two weeks and edits fields (`ThreadFields`); logged numbers stay out of the thread's log.
- It's on every app page: `AskMaru` (in `AppShell`) opens today's conversation in a panel, by its button or ⌘K / Ctrl+K; home has the conversation on the page. The panel sends the path as `where` (it never offers to open the page you're on), and on phones it steps aside when it opens a page. Other people's words can steer a model, so while their posts are part of a turn only reading, drafting replies and offering buttons run on the model's word; anything else it calls becomes a button the writer taps (`tapOnly` in `tools.ts`), and so do follow, unfollow and putting a thread on the profile, always. A tool the model wasn't given that round never runs. Drafts others will see are written without memories, pages or thread notes in the prompt, and one that carries a private word anyway is refused (`privateWord`). Moments carry `data-screen-post`; every message sends the ids of the posts mostly in view and the one you're on (`components/shared/screen.ts`: last under the pointer, else the most visible), and the server reads them itself (`screenOf`, only what the writer may see) but gives them to the model only when the turn is about them (`aboutScreen`: pointing words, an author's name, "he" or "she" when asked from a post, or any of the last four messages being about one). A tapped answer (`say:`) carries the screen like typed words. Wanting to answer a post drafts replies at once; a question about a post ("what do you think?") gets Maru's take in words with no tools (`tool_choice: "none"`), then taps to draft. `draft_replies` without the author's handle can only mean the post they're on. The face button on someone's moment (`askAbout`) opens the conversation about that post, with prompt chips. `draft_replies` must name the post's author as well as its number, so a reply can't land on the wrong post.
- The brain (`lib/companion/brain.ts`): with `GROQ_API_KEY` set, a message goes to a model with a fixed persona and the tools in `lib/companion/tools.ts` (threads and their fields, memories, page search, today's page and its questions, settings and names, people, opening any page, `offer_choices`), then one message: what Maru knows right now (names, calendar, threads with their latest values, memories, moods, the last three pages, the daily page's questions, the screen when it matters), the last eight messages as background marked already dealt with, then what they said. Keep the history out of chat turns: as turns, anything half-done in them reads as still being asked for, and the model goes back to it instead of answering. The persona and tools come first and stay the same, in a fixed order, so Groq caches them, and cached tokens don't count against its limits: keep anything that changes out of the system message. A keyword router (`toolsFor`) sends only the groups a message (and the one before it) points to, since each definition costs tokens; a pure question gets read-only tools.
- A reliable reply comes from code as well as the persona, because the same request can go either way. Asked something, it answers in words: a question answered with only a button gets one more call with tools off, and the button stays. It can't bring up a thread their message has nothing to do with (`strayThread`: an offer, or a log with no number in what they said; asking how it's all going, `asksAboutAll`, is about every thread), or "create" one it already keeps with nothing new; blank arguments never clear anything. Models put a button's thread under another key ("thread_id") or only on its label ("Mark scrapers done", no arguments), so a button on a thread is put right when it's offered and again when it's tapped (`threadChoice`); one that names no thread they keep, or would change nothing, isn't offered. A caption above buttons has to be a question, or it reads like a label ("Create a new habit"). "Walked 30 min" starts the thread and logs it (`valuesIn`), so nothing is left half-done.
- Honest and cheap by construction: a tool that can't do it throws `Unable`, so the model reads why; when every tool succeeds the turn ends there, since the receipts say it. One more round only when a tool failed or found something to read, a message with several things got just one after a first round that worked, a "can't" needs every tool, a reply claims an action no tool took (`tool_choice: "required"`), the model said nothing (it must offer choices), or it wrote itself a note about what to offer ("Offer to add a reminder…": `clean` cuts the note, and one round offers it as a choice while the rest of the reply stays). "Open my letters" is matched in code (`placeAsked`) with no model, and so is "how are we doing today?" (`statusNow`): one true sentence from code (what's due, past its date, stuck, the habits left today) and the threads that most need a word as their pieces, in the same order. A model reading the same facts once said a habit was up to date when it wasn't. Client actions come back with the reply: navigate, theme, refresh, `replied`, and `page-write`, which an open `EntryForm` appends through the `maru:page-write` event. Scripted check-in replies stay on the rules engine (`engine.ts`), which also answers news when no key is set or no model can take the turn.
- Groq calls go through `complete()` in `lib/ai/groq.ts`, which walks a list of models (free-tier limits are per model: 1,000 requests a day and 8,000 tokens a minute each) and falls back on rate limits, retired models, timeouts and tool calls Groq rejects; a conversation's first call waits once (up to six seconds) when every model is out of its per-minute tokens. The conversation uses `TOOL_MODELS` (gpt-oss first: Qwen without thinking often answers "Done." without calling the tool). Pages are read with the model on the writer's own save and at most every ten minutes while autosaving (`lib/companion/pages.ts`). Every call a writer starts (chat rounds, page reads, takes, layout ideas) passes `by` and counts against their day: 150 model calls (`lib/ai/budget.ts`, a counter on `User`), so one account or a script with its session can't spend the shared key for everyone; over it, chat says so in one line (`spent`) and everything else does without, as when Groq is busy. A later round that fails keeps what earlier rounds did. Check-in wording is code's own: no model on the day's first visit.

## Sign-up, pages and messages

- Onboarding asks what fills someone's days, not only their job: fifteen roles (`ROLE_KEYS` in `lib/types.ts`), from developer and teacher to shift work, parent or carer, training for something, looking after myself and retired. Each has a starting page (`lib/roles.ts`), things people in it tend to have going on and topics (`components/onboarding/script.ts`), opening questions (`lib/companion/copy.ts`) and questions in the bank. The topic chips come from the bank's tags, so a new topic needs questions behind it (`lib/questions/bank.ts`; ids are stored in saved pages, so never rename one).
- A writer's page layouts all stay: making, copying or switching never removes one. "Your pages" at the top of the gallery lists them (`myPages` in `lib/templates/mine.ts`, `GET /api/templates`), switches today's page (`POST /api/templates/[id]/use`, or `use_page` in chat) and deletes any but today's. A saved page keeps its own questions and answers, and saving today's page keeps answers to questions the layout no longer asks (`savePage`), which the writing page shows as "Earlier today". The writing page sends only what changed since it loaded or last saved, and the server keeps every answer not sent, so a tab left open on another device can't erase what was written since. Everything a writer stores has a size (`lib/validation/schemas.ts`: an answer 40,000 characters, a page 60,000, a layout's settings only the keys `FieldConfig` has), a new page can only be about the last two weeks, and a writer keeps at most 60 layouts (`PAGES_MOST`; copying counts, and a copy raises a gallery page's count once per writer), so one account can't fill the free database.
- Accounts start unlisted (`User.isPublic`), and nobody is listed without choosing it: `PublicSwitch` is on onboarding's handle step, off by default, and in Settings. Listed means name search and suggestions, so say that wherever someone chooses it. A whole handle finds anyone (`discoverPeople`), which is why "Pick a handle so friends can find you" is true either way; a partial one doesn't.
- People is your people, not everyone: the People feed and home show you and your people (`yourPeople` in `lib/social/visibility.ts`): everyone you follow until you pick some, then only the ones you picked (`Follow.close`, the star on a profile or in the chooser above the feed, `PATCH /api/follow`). There's no feed of strangers; anyone else is found by name in Find people, which also has suggestions.
- A profile is shared as `/@handle` (`ShareProfile`; the proxy rewrites it to `app/share/[handle]`, and `/u/handle` opened signed out shows the same): a card anyone can see, signed in or not, with only what a profile shows everyone (`shareCard`: name, handle, role, bio), a picture for chats (`opengraph-image.tsx`) and no search indexing. Joining from it (`/register?follow=`) ends setup on that profile.
- Messages are one conversation per pair (`Conversation`, `DirectMessage`, rules in `lib/social/messages.ts`), and only between people who follow each other: following is one tap, then the other follows back, and only then can either write. If one stops following, the conversation stays to read and nothing new can be sent; a closed conversation says what's missing and offers the follow (`closedReason`). Either person can block the other, from a conversation or a profile (pressed twice). A block cuts everything between them (`blockedBetween` in `lib/social/visibility.ts`): posts and profiles both ways, replies, reactions, follows, notifications, messages, presence and shared threads, and it ends both follows, so unblocking doesn't bring them back. A block made before anyone wrote is only on the blocker's list. `/messages` lists conversations and `/messages/[handle]` is one (both panes on a desktop); an open conversation checks for new messages only while visible: every 2 seconds while they're typing or a message came in the last minute, every 5 while they're here, every 15 when they aren't, and every 30 once it's closed or nobody here has touched the page for two minutes. A message is words or one of Kata's stickers (`lib/stickers.ts`, drawn by `Sticker`; ids are stored, so never rename one), sent from the picker under the box (`Picker`: an everyday set of emoji that go in where the cursor is, and the stickers, which send at once). Nothing is ever uploaded: no pictures, and a message of one to three emoji is just drawn big. Friends see when the other is here ("Active now", from `User.lastSeenAt`) and typing (`User.typingTo` / `typingAt` on the typist, one chat at a time, pinged every few seconds at most while they type and cleared by sending), only while both show it (`User.showActive`, the switch in Settings: off hides yours and theirs) and never across a block (`seesActivity` in `lib/social/presence.ts`).
- A thread can be shared with a few friends (`Thread.with`, at most 12; rules in `lib/social/sharing.ts`): people who follow each other, never across a block, picked on the thread's own page (`/threads/[id]`, `SharedThread`; Share in the Threads page's editor opens it with `?share=1`). They see its name, where it stands and everything added to it (`ThreadUpdate`: updates, comments, cheers and lines about who's on it), never its notes, next step, tags or numbers, and they add their own; only its writer sets where it stands, and a step set anywhere lands on it with no words (`recordStep`, from `saveThread`; taking a habit's day back takes its step back). Someone added later sees what's already there; someone taken off, or who leaves, stops seeing it at once, and what they added stays. Archived, it's the writer's alone until it comes back. Threads shared with you are listed on the Threads page (`sharedWithMe`). Being added, updates, comments and cheers are notifications (`shared`, `update`, `comment`, `cheer`, keyed by `threadId`), dropped once the thread isn't theirs to see, and opening the thread reads them (`seenThread`). Maru only opens its page (`open_page` with a thread) and never shares one or adds anything to it. A block, or no longer following each other, takes each person off the other's threads. One person adds at most 30 updates a day to a thread.
- The shell's pulse (`/api/pulse`, `usePulse` in `AppShell`) keeps the bell and the inbox current: every 10 seconds while someone's using Kata, every 30 once they've gone quiet, not at all after half an hour untouched (so a tab left open doesn't say "Active now" all night), never in a hidden tab, and at once when they come back, touch it or change page (or on `PULSE_EVENT`). A follow, reaction or cheer taken back leaves its notification read and hidden (`gone`), so flicking it on and off can't ring anyone's bell again. A notification that arrives while Kata is open shows as a toast, and the notifications page takes it in, as a shared thread's page takes in news about itself (a refresh, no toast). The same check keeps `lastSeenAt`, writing at most once a minute, so it's three small indexed reads most of the time. Keep it that cheap; besides it, only an open conversation and the list beside it poll. Profiles show Message only to friends, and Maru drafts messages (`draft_message`, friends only) that go only on the writer's tap (`send_message`). A credited gallery page (`Template.credit`) links to its maker's profile.
- Settings ends with Start fresh and Delete account (`StartOver`, rules in `lib/account`). Each asks a few questions in a dialog and needs the password again. Start fresh clears what the writer picks (pages and letters, threads and lists, Maru's conversation and memories, posts and replies; breakdowns go with pages or threads) and keeps the account, profile, handle, people, messages and page layouts, and can send them back through setup. Deleting first offers start fresh instead, then a copy of everything they made (`GET /api/account/export`, one Markdown file: pages, threads with their notes and numbers, memories, lists, letters, posts and replies, asks, and the messages they sent), then says exactly what goes: everything, their conversations for both people included, since a conversation is addressed by a handle that's gone. Either way, what they left on other people's posts comes off with it and everyone else's counts stay true. Their threads take everything friends added to them; what they added to friends' shared threads stays on start fresh, like messages, and goes with the account. A deleted account lands on `/bye`, and the app layout sends any other session whose account is gone there too, which signs it out. A new password (changed or reset) ends every other session within a minute: the token carries `User.sessionVersion`, which the auth `jwt` callback checks (`lib/auth/sessions.ts`, read at most once a minute per account). A session whose account was deleted is marked `gone` there: it has no user id, so every API answers 401, and the proxy sends it to `/bye`. Ten wrong passwords in fifteen minutes and sign-in, and the password check on change-password, wait out the rest, counted on the account so it holds without Upstash. Only the newest reset link works, and an address gets at most one every two minutes.

## Breakdowns

- A breakdown is how a stretch of time went, worked out in code from what the writer logged (`gather` in `lib/breakdowns/stats.ts`): threads done, moving (updated and not stuck at the end) and stuck, threads started, habit days, logged numbers, pages, moods and posts, the page's own energy and numbers (`answers`, by the question's wording: hours slept, minutes trained), and the lists for its days (tasks done, time in focus). A task done on a list is something logged that day. Breakdowns made before a figure was counted don't have it, so read new ones with a default. Habit days come from `Thread.doneDays` (kept by `habitDone`, so pass it the whole thread), the run a streak ends on, and days with numbers. No model decides any of it, so the same stretch always gives the same breakdown.
- The score out of 10 (`scoreOf`) has three parts, all shown: days with something logged, open threads finished or moved (a week asks four of them, longer stretches more), and days habits were kept. A part with nothing to go on is left out; nothing logged means no score. Today only counts once something happened in it. The page says how it's worked out: never present it as Maru's judgement.
- Stretches are the writer's local days, read in code (`parsePeriod` in `lib/breakdowns/period.ts`: "last month", "since Oct 3", "October", "2026-10-01 to 2026-10-15"; weeks start on Monday) and named from the day they're viewed (`periodTitle`: "This week so far", "Last month").
- Weekly ones are made the first time the writer turns up after a week ends (`ensureWeekly`, from the day's first greeting, which carries it as a receipt, or the Breakdowns page); a week with nothing in it is kept but never listed, and a week they barely had isn't scored. Asked ones come from the page's chips or from Maru (`make_breakdown`; with no stretch named it offers this week, last week, this month and a form for any other) and go after thirty days unless kept (a TTL index on `expiresAt`). Asking for a finished week that has its weekly one brings that one up to date.
- Maru's take, two or three sentences, is written once, the first time a breakdown is opened (`writeTake`, `POST /api/breakdowns/[id]/take`). It gets the facts in words without the number, or it repeats the score.
- "Give me a breakdown", "break down last month" and "how was my week" are matched in code (`breakdownAsked`) with no model. Anything else goes to the model, and the keyword router adds `make_breakdown` even to questions. A reply that reads the breakdown back is dropped: the receipt says it.
- Patterns (`lib/patterns`) are what went with better or worse moods and energy over the last 60 days that are over (today isn't, so the window ends yesterday), worked out in code: `patternsOf` in `compute.ts` (client-safe) on the days `loadPatterns` reads. A day's mood is its page's own mood answer, else the last mood check-in that day (`dayMoods`, which breakdowns read the same way); its energy is the page's energy answer. Everything kept splits the days in two: a habit done or not, a number at or above its split (its goal when both sides get four days, else as evenly as its values allow), a page's yes-or-no question, one of its six most-used tags, and moving a thread that isn't a habit forward. Each side needs four days with a mood. Clear is half a step and three standard errors apart (Welch's t, with a floor under the spread so four identical days aren't certainty), worth watching two and a half; a writer has a dozen comparisons, and a looser bar would show one made by chance most of the time. The words say what went together, never what caused what (`patternSaid`).
- The Patterns card (`PatternsCard` + `PatternRows`) is on Breakdowns after the latest one, at `#patterns`. Under eight days with a mood it says how many so far, what makes a day count, and shows an example labelled made up. "What makes my days better?" and "does walking help my mood?" are matched in code (`patternsAsked`, `patternsNow`), not in focus, and answered in one sentence (`patternsReply`: the thing they named, else the strongest clear pattern, energy first when asked about energy) with a Patterns receipt; the persona never claims a pattern.

## Missions and focus

- A mission (`lib/db/models/Mission.ts`) is a temporary list: a to-do list for today, or a push on something for as long as it takes. Its tasks are temporary threads: they live on the mission, never on the Threads page, until the writer keeps the unfinished ones as threads (`keepAsThreads`). One document holds the lot (tasks, timers, a log of what happened, capped at 80 lines), so Maru reads it in one query and it stays small; a task stores only the fields it uses. Missions stay when finished or dropped, to report on and to run again (`runAgain`); only Delete on a finished one removes it.
- Timers are "time so far" plus "running since" (`spentMs` / `startedAt`, `focusMs` / `focusedAt`), so nothing ticks on the server and a closed tab loses nothing. Every change goes through `apply` (`lib/missions/apply.ts`), a pure step the page, Maru and the tests share; `changeMission` saves it against `updatedAt`, so two tabs can't overwrite each other. A focus untouched for six hours was left on: `focusedMission` stops the clocks at the last thing that happened and the missions page says so, so a night never counts as work.
- Tasks are read in code from how people say them (`parseTask` in `lib/missions/parse.ts`: "Gym at 6pm for 45 min", "Meeting 3-4pm", "half an hour"), so the model passes the words along and the tool definitions stay short. Clock times are read the way a to-do list means them (`clockAt`): "at 3" in the afternoon is 3pm, "at 9" whichever 9 comes next, "at 1" late at night is 1am, and on a list for tomorrow 7 to 11 are the morning; "7.30pm" is half past seven, and "1.5 hours" no clock time. A list made with no day before four in the morning is for the day ahead (`listDay`), as a plan is. A list named by its day is called from today (`missionName`: "Today’s list", "Yesterday’s list"); yesterday's that's still going is offered to carry over (`carry`), to the day a new list would be for (`listDay`), which drops its clock times. Maru never adds a task that's already open on the list said another way ("Call bank" for "Call the bank", `alreadyOn`), and leaves out a button that would; the page's own Add takes what's typed.
- Planning a day is code (`lib/missions/plan.ts`): what's due that day, then what's past its date (not while waiting, at most a week back), the habits their rhythm asks for that day, and the two freshest next steps on what's moving, six at most. Times start at the next quarter hour ten minutes out (a day ahead at nine), one after another with a fifteen-minute break, and nothing starts after ten at night. Plain "plan my day" means the rest of today until six, and the day ahead from then until the day turns. Kata can't see a calendar, so the times are suggestions and the plan says so. It's offered as one button (`planTurn`): `start_mission` with the tasks in the words a list reads ("Finish scrapers at 2:30pm for 60 min") and the rows to draw (`plan` on the choice, drawn by `PlanOffer` in `Choices`); tasks keep their threads' titles, so done on the list counts there. "Plan my day", "plan tomorrow" and "what should I do today?" are matched in code (`planAsked`, `planNow`); the model has `plan_day` and never writes a plan itself. The hello's last offer is "Plan my day" (after six, "Plan tomorrow").
- Asks (`lib/companion/asks.ts`, `Ask`) are things the writer asked to be asked about at a time: "remind me to call Mum tonight", "ask me about the dentist on Friday". The time is read in code (`askWhen`: tonight is eight, tomorrow nine, "in 2 hours", "on Friday at 6pm", a time missed by more than an hour means its next one, so "at 9am" said at two is tomorrow morning, and one just missed means half an hour from now), and so is the whole request (`askAsked`, `askNow`; the model has `ask_later`). Missing what or when, the form asks. It comes up as Maru's message the first time they're in Kata after its time, with Done, In an hour, Tomorrow and Drop it, handled in `answer` with no model. `User.nextAskAt` keeps the earliest one waiting: the layout hands it to `AskMaru`, which sets a timer and calls `POST /api/companion/asks` when it's due (in a hidden tab, once they look; never while they're writing), and the pulse never reads it. The hello brings up what's due first. Each ask is claimed before it's written, so nothing comes up twice. Twenty can wait at once; Settings lists them under Waiting to ask, each with Drop.
- Focus (`User.focusMissionId`, one mission at a time) hands the app over to it: `AppShell` drops the rail and tab bar for `FocusBar`, Home goes to `/focus`, and the whole app wears the focus colours (`data-focus` on the shell and `<html>`: Deep sea, Moss or Ember, `User.focusTheme`, picked on the focus screen). They're dark in either theme, with quieter outlines. Stop focus keeps the list and brings everything back. Nothing pops up over focus: the pulse still counts, but shows no toast. A focus change made in the browser shows at once (`focusChanged`), then the server's word replaces it.
- In focus Maru asks only about the mission, from the clock, while Kata is open (`promptFor` in `lib/missions/prompts.ts`): a timer ran out, a task's time came, 25 minutes on one thing, ten idle. Nothing is stored until there's an answer (`askedAt`), and a chime only plays after a tap has woken the page's sound. The day's opening waits until focus ends (`ensureToday`), the model gets the mission instead of the usual facts (`focusFacts`) and a short tool list, and most of what people say in focus is settled in code with no model (`whatAsked` in `lib/missions/said.ts`, `missionTurn`): a new list, focus on and off, where are we, what's next, done, skip, start, pause, stuck, add, wrap up, kill it, a report. "Done" and its kind only mean this in focus; "focus on X" only starts focus when X is on the list, since "focus on my health" is a goal.
- The report is worked out in code (`reportOf`): done of total, time in focus, where the time went against each timer, what's left. Maru's take is written once, the first time it's opened (`writeTake` in `lib/missions`).

## The database

- Mongo never changes an existing index, and mongoose doesn't tell anyone when it can't build one: change an index's rules in a schema and the database keeps the old ones until something fails for writers. `repairIndexes` (`lib/db/indexes.ts`) runs once per instance, from `connectDB`, and replaces an index that has the same keys and different rules; it leaves anything no schema mentions, and skips previews, which share the live database. A new model goes in its list.
- A query by handle goes through `byHandle` / `byHandles` (`lib/social/handles.ts`): the handle index is partial (only string handles), and a plain `{ handle }` doesn't match its filter, so it read every user.
- `GET /api/health` says whether the database answers and its indexes match the code. Check it after a deploy that touches a schema.
- Queries wait for the connection (`bufferCommands` on, up to `bufferTimeoutMS` 8s, in `lib/db/mongoose.ts`). Pages load in parallel, and on a cold instance a loader's first query can come before `connectDB` finishes: with buffering off that threw, and People said "can't reach the database" on the first visit after a quiet spell. Still `await connectDB()` before a page's parallel loads. A page's catch-all error says the database, so check the server log for the real error before believing it.
- The live database is a free Atlas cluster, which pauses after long inactivity: its address then stops resolving (`querySrv ENOTFOUND`). The daily cron connects, which keeps it awake. It also prunes the conversation (`pruneConversations`: Maru's messages after 90 days, its check-ins kept, buttons and receipts after two weeks; nothing the writer said) and writes the week's and month's letters in batches, each claimed first, so whoever one run misses the next one reaches and none is written twice. Previews share this database, so they never build indexes or collections in it (`autoIndex` and `autoCreate` off). There are no backups on the free tier: take one by hand (mongodump) before anything risky.

## Days and time

Think through a real person's day before writing anything that depends on the clock: 7am, 2pm, 9pm and 1:30am, a Sunday, someone back after three days away.

- A writer's day ends when they go to bed, not at midnight: it turns at 4am in their own zone (`localDay` in `lib/companion/time.ts`). Everything a writer sees uses that day; a UTC day is only for the letters' schedule. Their zone follows their device (`AppShell`).
- A page is about a day. Before noon it opens on yesterday until yesterday's is written (`pageDayFor`, `pageState`), because that's the day that has happened; `/entry/new?day=today` or `yesterday` picks one, and yesterday stays open. Entry.date is the day it's about, not when it was typed: reason from that, not `createdAt`.
- Pages hold anyone's wording, so a question's own words say when it makes sense (`lib/questions/tense.ts`). On yesterday's page what looks back reads that way (`asYesterday`), for showing only: a page stores its words as they were. On today's page what can't be answered yet waits under "Later today" (`tooEarly`) and can still be answered. Maru's own questions go by the clock, not the check-in setting.
- A streak is worked out from the days written (`streakFromDates`), never added to, because yesterday's page can be written after today's. Don't show a stored one.
- A habit's title can say how often it's meant ("Gym 3x a week"). `habitGap` gives the days it can go without breaking its run, so rest days aren't asked about and aren't missed days, and `habitName` takes the frequency out of a sentence. "Walked 30 min yesterday" counts yesterday (`habitDone` takes a day).
- On a weekend (`isDayOff`; not for shift workers and healthcare, whose days off move), bank questions marked `workday` don't come up, and a page's own questions about the job (`workdayOnly`) step aside under "For a working day" with Answer now, because plenty of people work Saturdays.
- A bank question comes up on any day of the week, in a box to write in. Looking back say "lately" and looking ahead "in the next few days", not "this week", which means nothing on a Monday morning or a Sunday night. Don't presume it happened, and say who "they" are: it's alone on its card.
- Nothing is sent outside Kata: a check-in or an ask waits for them to open it, so never write copy that implies a notification, and never the word "remind". Asked to be reminded, Maru keeps an ask and says when it'll come up while they're in Kata.

## Layout and copy

- Gutter `px-margin`; signed-in pages sit in `AppPage`. A new signed-in route also goes in `PROTECTED` in `proxy.ts`: pages read `session!`, and the layout's redirect doesn't stop them rendering. `AppShell` gives a desktop rail and, below `lg`, a top bar and a bottom tab bar. Touch targets are at least 44px. Single-column grids use `grid-cols-1` so wide children can't widen the page. No horizontal scroll at any width.
- Copy is short, specific and true, in sentence case, no exclamation marks. Say what really happens: pages are read by an AI model run by Groq, so "no one else on Kata", never "only you". No invented proof (users, quotes, counts) on public pages; demo content is labelled as an example.
- Kata is for anyone's days, not only people at desks: don't write copy that assumes a job, a project, children or a partner.
